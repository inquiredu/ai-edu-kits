# Worked example (synthetic)

Everything in this folder is invented. Lakeview Public Schools does not exist, and no response here came from a real person. The input is small on purpose — sixteen responses — so you can check every count yourself.

The first-pass synthesis contains six deliberate errors of the kind these tools actually make: a count inflated in the summary, a response folded into a theme it argued against, a two-person cluster promoted to a theme, one voice turned into a group view, a dissenting response dropped entirely, and an unsourced research claim. The draft inherits some of them and adds one of its own. Then the audit catches them.

Read in this order:

1. `input-set.md` — what staff said
2. `02-synthesis-first-pass.md` — what the Synthesizer produced before review
3. `03-draft-excerpt.md` — part of what the Drafter built on it
4. `04-audit.md` — what the independent Auditor found

## What a corrected snapshot looks like

After the synthesis was re-run with the audit flags as notes:

> Sixteen middle school staff responded. The most common request, from 5 of 16, was clear and consistent AI expectations on each assignment (R01, R04, R07, R10, R13); two of the five named a shared scale or label (R04, R10). Three asked for unevaluated time to learn the tools, alone or with colleagues (R02, R08, R12), and three asked that productive struggle and student voice stay protected (R03, R09, R14); R14 said explicitly that this was not a cheating concern. One respondent does not want AI in their classroom this year and asked that this be an acceptable choice (R15). One special education respondent described AI as both an accommodation for some students and a risk of hiding learning needs for others, and asked for help telling the difference (R06).

Notice what changed: every sentence carries its count, the dissent is present in its own words, and the special education view keeps both of its halves.
