# Guardrails

Staff feedback feels low-risk until someone reads a summary and recognizes a colleague. These rules exist so that never happens because of a tool.

## Which lane are you in?

**Approved lane.** Your district's approved AI tool, covered by a data agreement you have confirmed, used under your local AI and data policy. Identifiable staff input may be permissible here, but "permissible" is a floor. Still remove names unless there is a reason to keep them.

**Unapproved lane.** Any tool without a confirmed agreement — including personal accounts on otherwise-familiar tools. Only fully de-identified input goes in. No exceptions for "just this once."

If you are not sure which lane you are in, you are in the unapproved lane.

**Student information never goes into this kit.** It is built for staff and adult input. If comments mention specific students, remove those details before any tool sees them, in either lane.

## De-identification (Gate 1, done by a person)

Before input reaches any AI role:

- Remove names, emails, and signatures.
- Remove or generalize details that point to one person: "as the only Spanish teacher," "after my surgery," a specific room number or incident.
- **Small-group suppression.** A role label with fewer than `{{MIN_GROUP_SIZE}}` respondents identifies people. Merge small groups into a broader label ("Specialists," "Other staff") unless respondents agreed in advance to role attribution. Record that agreement in your inputs file.
- Keep respondent IDs (R01, R02…) so every claim can be traced back without knowing who said it.

The Preparer role flags what you may have missed. It does not replace you doing this step.

## What the AI roles may and may not do

| May | May not |
|---|---|
| Sort, count, and quote from the input | Characterize what respondents "really" feel |
| Name tensions and single voices | Merge a single voice into a group claim |
| Draft framework language | Supply research, statistics, or citations not provided in an evidence pack |
| Write research questions for a person to answer | Decide priorities or next steps |
| Flag possible identifiers | Delete or rewrite respondent text |

## Evidence pack (optional)

If you want the Drafter to connect themes to research, give it an evidence pack: sources a person on your team has read and vetted, with the relevant passage. The Drafter may cite only from that pack. Anything else becomes a research question, not a claim.

## Tone

Findings are written as challenge, never threat. No alarm as a rhetorical device, no framing a staff group as resistant, behind, or the problem. The auditor checks for this.
