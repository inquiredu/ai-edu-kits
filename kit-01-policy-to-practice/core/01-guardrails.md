# Guardrails

Board policies are public documents, so the data risk here is low. The accuracy risk is high: a translation that misstates policy becomes the de facto policy in every building that uses it.

## Tool lane
Adopted policy text is public and can go into any tool. Draft policy, legal memos, attorney communications, and anything marked confidential go only into your approved tool, if at all. Attorney–client communications should not go into AI tools without counsel's agreement.

## The three tags

Every statement in a translation carries one:

- **[Text §X]** — the policy says this. The paraphrase must not add or remove obligation.
- **[Interpretation §X — ruled by {{POLICY_OWNER}}]** — the policy is ambiguous or silent here, and the named owner has decided how the district reads it. The Translator may use only rulings recorded at Gate 2.
- **[Guidance: document name]** — comes from a guideline, procedure, or handbook, not the policy itself. Readers need to know guidance can change without a board vote.

## Modal verbs are load-bearing

"Must," "shall," "may," "should," and "is encouraged to" carry different obligations. The Translator preserves them exactly. Changing "may" to "must," or dropping a "must" to "should," is a HIGH flag.

## What the AI roles may and may not do

| May | May not |
|---|---|
| Map clauses and quote them | Resolve ambiguity on its own authority |
| Point out conflicts between sections | Offer legal conclusions |
| Draft plain-language versions | Add requirements that are not in the text |
| Suggest questions for the policy owner | Present interpretation as text |

## Tone
Administrators are peers and site experts. Staff are professionals with reasons for their questions. No talking down, no compliance-threat framing, no alarm.
