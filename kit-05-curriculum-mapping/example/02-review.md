# Pedagogy review (example)

## A. Moments

**RL.7.1 — CUT as written; FIX possible**
- Lens 2, HIGH: the standard's verb is "cite several pieces of textual evidence." The AI does the citing. Discussing whether AI-found evidence fits is a different, easier task: evaluating a finished selection rather than searching the text and choosing.
- Lens 9, MEDIUM: "increases engagement and deepens comprehension" has no source in the evidence pack.
- Lens 3, LOW: shared devices are a reasonable access plan; name a role for students who aren't at the keyboard.
- Lenses 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8: no issues found.
- Smallest fix: reverse the order. Students find and cite their own evidence first (Level 0). Then, at Level 1, the AI offers feedback on the student-made analysis — for example, asking which inference is least supported. The citing stays with the student. Remove the effect claim or convert it: "Does feedback after independent citation change how students revise their inferences?"

**RI.7.8 — CUT as written; strong idea worth saving**
- Lens 6, HIGH: students produce a deepfake of a real public figure. The guardrails prohibit students producing deepfakes, and this also misrepresents a real person.
- Lens 5, HIGH: involves a real person's likeness; video generation is likely outside the approved tool's scope for students.
- Lens 1, MEDIUM: fabricated media of a public figure can be divisive in a classroom.
- Lens 2: passes — the tracing and evaluating stay with the student.
- Smallest fix: keep Level 3, change the artifact. The teacher prepares a clearly labeled, AI-generated short opinion piece on a school-relevant topic, with one claim supported by insufficient evidence and one by irrelevant evidence, and no real people. Students trace the argument and decide which claims are unsupported and why. This hits every verb in RI.7.8 and builds the grade 7 progression goal of understanding how models produce text that sounds convincing.

## B. "No AI" recommendations
None made. Worth asking whether RL.7.1 should be a "No AI moment" entirely — the fix above keeps AI only after the student has done the standard's work.

## C. Patterns
Both moments started from what the tool can produce rather than from the standard's verb. When the first question is "what can AI make here?", lens 2 and lens 6 failures follow.

## D. Questions for the curriculum lead
- For RL.7.1, is Level 1 feedback after independent citation worth the class time, or should this standard stay AI-free at grade 7?
- The revised RI.7.8 moment asks teachers to prepare a flawed AI-generated text. Should a shared bank of these be built once, rather than by each teacher?
